Winning back 7 million monthly visits after a core update

After a Google core update, ScrapeHero's monthly traffic fell from 10M to 3M in under three months. Five months into recovery, it was back at 10M.

Product
ScrapeHero, web scraping and data services
Audience
US business and developer buyers
My role
Owned SEO and AI search
Measured in
Search Console, with GA4
ContextProblemWhat I didResult

Context

ScrapeHero sells web scraping and data services to US businesses and developers. I own SEO across four lines: web crawling services, custom APIs, ScrapeHero Cloud scrapers and Data Store datasets. I also manage SEO for the POI dataset marketplace.

Organic search was a major lead channel for the business, so traffic wasn't a vanity number here.

The problem

A Google core update took monthly traffic from about 10M visits to 3M in under three months.

The first job was making sure this was a sustained decline and not the usual swing after an update. Once it held, I moved to recovery. Along the way we found faulty links in the programmatic report pages, and I worked with the team on the fixes.

What I did

  1. Confirmed the drop was realChecked that the decline was holding before changing anything big.
  2. Fixed what was brokenWorked with the team on faulty links in the programmatic report pages.
  3. Mapped intent across the siteKeywords and search intent across tutorials, around 24 blog posts a month and service pages, so informational, comparison and buying queries each had a clear home.
  4. Built topic clusters by service segmentClusters around web scraping and data extraction, across multiple service lines.
  5. Ran outreach and link buildingConsistent blog outreach and backlink building, with backlink audits in Semrush and Ahrefs.

Result

Recovery timeline

3M at the low point · ~7.8M by month three · 10M by month five.

The recovery came from all of this together. I can't split it into how much each piece contributed, and I'm not claiming a lead or revenue number from it here.

Rewriting bottom-of-funnel posts so AI Overviews quote them

ScrapeHero's long-form BOFU articles had limited visibility in Google AI Overviews. A set of shorter, answer-first posts was followed by a 200% rise in AI Overview citations.

Product
ScrapeHero
Content
BOFU blog posts
My role
Owned the approach and format
Where
Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity
ProblemThe new formatResult

The problem

BOFU means bottom of funnel: posts for people who are close to buying and comparing their options. ScrapeHero had long-form articles for these queries, and they weren't showing up much in AI Overviews.

Long guides are good for reading. They're harder for an AI system to pull one clean answer out of.

The new format

  • Shorter posts. A dedicated set of 500 to 700 word BOFU posts, separate from the long guides.
  • Answer first. Each post answers the user's query directly, then supports it.
  • Phrase-based. Written around the phrases people actually search and ask, instead of one broad keyword.
  • Structured. Schema markup and answer-ready structure on tutorials and guides, so answer engines can surface and cite them.

Result

After the change

+200% AI Overview citations for BOFU posts. ScrapeHero also started getting cited in ChatGPT and Gemini.

I track citations going forward with the weekly n8n check described in the workflow case study.

Two workflows that solve two different problems. One brings four SEO data sources into a single analysis. The other checks every week whether AI answers cite ScrapeHero.

Why I built it

Rankings live in Semrush. Traffic lives in GA4. Queries live in Search Console. Behavior lives in Clarity. Each one answers a different question, and switching between four tools to answer one makes it easy to miss how they connect.

Checking AI answers for citations by hand is the other problem. It's slow and nobody does it consistently.

How it works

  • Day to day, I use Claude and ChatGPT for keyword research, search intent analysis and SEO content support.
  • The workflows collect and line up the data. Deciding what to fix next is still my call.

Where it stands

Status

The citation check runs every week for ScrapeHero. The n8n and Claude agents also handle repetitive SEO tasks, and I'm extending them to cover more of the routine work.

MetricsCart sells digital shelf analytics to consumer brands and e-commerce agencies. Its buyers do a lot of research before they talk to sales. The goal was to be the source Google and AI tools reach for at that stage.

Product

ProblemArchitectureStatus

The problem

People researching digital shelf analytics look for definitions first, then compare vendors, then build a shortlist. If MetricsCart isn't there at each step, a competitor is. Organic search was a major lead channel here too.

The architecture

  • Insights covers the research stage, from "what is this" to "which tool".
  • Solution pages stay focused on the buyer's job instead of feature lists.
  • AEO and GEO on the comparison and definition pages, so AI search tools can extract and cite them.
